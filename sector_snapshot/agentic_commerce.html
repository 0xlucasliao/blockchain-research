Agentic commerce

April 2026 · x402 processed 75M transactions in 30 days. Mastercard's first live agentic transaction: September 29 2025. McKinsey projects $3–5T in agent-driven commerce by 2030. This is what's shippable today — and what's still gated.

High demand · not yet practical

Agent uses your credit card fully unattended at any merchant — and disputes go wrong purchases automatically
Unattended credit card spend at arbitrary Web2 merchants · no human-present 3DS · Reg E / FCBA protection expected

Why blocked: Mastercard Agent Pay and Visa VIC solve this for their integrated issuer/merchant partners — a growing but still small fraction of all merchants. For the majority: (1) 3DS human-present authentication cannot be solved by an LLM. (2) An agent with raw PAN + CVV in its context is a prompt-injection attack waiting to happen. (3) California AB 316 (January 2026) establishes the human principal is liable for all agent actions — but neither card network rules nor consumer protection law has defined the dispute rights when an agent makes a mistake. Friendly fraud chargebacks from agent-initiated transactions are expected to be statistically visible in high-risk merchant data by 2026–2028.

Agent negotiates price, wins a bid, and pays — all in one automated loop
Dynamic-price agent commerce · M2M negotiation → conditional payment → atomic settlement

Why blocked: ACP and MPP define checkout at a fixed, already-agreed price — not negotiation. No widely adopted M2M protocol defines how two AI agents negotiate price in real time. An agent bidding in an auction also needs a conditional payment primitive: escrow that releases only on win. x402 and MPP are request-and-pay; they don't support contingent authorization. Smart-contract escrow can model this in principle, but no major retail or B2B platform exposes a standard escrow API at the checkout layer.

Deploy an agent with a wallet and let it spend with absolutely no limits or oversight
Unrestricted autonomous agent spending · no session cap · no human mandate · no audit requirement

Why blocked: Three independent blockers. (1) LLMs are vulnerable to prompt injection in web pages, emails, and API responses — an agent with an unrestricted wallet in its context is a theft vector. (2) California AB 316 (January 2026) makes the human principal fully liable for all agent actions; unlimited agent spending = unlimited human liability with no insurance backstop. (3) No global Know Your Agent (KYA) regime exists: FATF Travel Rule obligations cannot be fulfilled without agent identity linked to a legal person. Every production agent payment system in April 2026 enforces a human-defined spending mandate. This is an architectural requirement, not a gap to design around.

One AI agent pays another AI agent for completed work — with no human in the compliance loop at all
Autonomous agent-to-agent payment · no human principal on either side · fully machine-to-machine settlement

Why blocked: Three layers. (1) Any value transfer above de minimis thresholds requires a KYC'd human principal under FinCEN / FCA / MAS rules. Fully principal-less agent-to-agent payments trigger unhosted wallet requirements — agents must be linked to a verified human (World AgentKit's explicit design thesis). (2) No standard exists for an orchestrator to verify work was completed before releasing payment — multi-agent frameworks have internal tracking but no payment-release hook. (3) On-chain payments are irreversible; agent-to-agent disputes have no contract law, no arbitration, and no escrow standard. Micropayments via x402 sessions are live; fully unaccountable M2M settlement is not.
