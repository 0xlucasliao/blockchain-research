High demand · not yet practical

Buy now, pay later — split a crypto purchase into four instalments
On-chain BNPL · no Web2 equivalent yet · Klarna / Afterpay / Affirm = $180B+ market

Why blocked: Three separate gaps stack. (1) Credit underwriting requires FICO/bureau data and identity binding — wallets are pseudonymous. (2) Chains are push-only; USDT (the largest consumer stablecoin) has no permit() standard to enable merchant-side pulls. (3) Default recovery requires legal recourse; pseudonymous wallets can be abandoned costlessly. Klarna's response: launch KlarnaUSD on Stripe's Tempo chain — a permissioned issuer-controlled stablecoin — which re-introduces off-chain identity, not an open-chain solution.

Tap your phone at any checkout terminal and pay directly in stablecoin
Tap-to-pay stablecoin at existing POS · no card scheme · NFC direct

Why blocked: The ~150M installed-base Visa/Mastercard-certified POS terminals run EMV kernels that only read ISO 7816 / 14443 card payloads. They physically cannot interpret a Solana Pay URL or EIP-3009 authorization. New crypto-capable hardware exists (Arx Burner Terminal, Ingenico × WalletConnect) but requires net-new terminal deployment or firmware updates — it cannot piggyback the existing installed base. On iPhone, Apple's Secure Element NFC remained closed to non-Apple-Pay flows in most markets until EU DMA pressure in 2025, but rollout is still limited.

Set up a monthly subscription that automatically charges your crypto wallet
Recurring pull payments from self-custody · merchant-initiated debit · ACH equivalent

Why blocked: Blockchains are push-only by design. USDC on EVM supports EIP-3009 transferWithAuthorization and EIP-2612 permit, enabling pull-style flows via relayers — but USDT (the largest retail stablecoin at ~$80B on Tron) explicitly does not implement these standards. Even with permit-enabled tokens: no native on-chain scheduler (chains don't run timers), no overdraft when the wallet is empty on charge day, and no ACH-style NSF retry. Stripe launched USDC subscription billing on Base/Polygon in October 2025 — live but limited to USDC-only, with explicit documentation that empty-wallet involuntary churn is a new uncovered category.

Dispute a crypto payment and get your money back if the merchant fails to deliver
Buyer protection / chargeback · on-chain finality conflict · Visa / PayPal Buyer Protection equivalent

Why blocked: Card chargebacks exist because card networks are private, contractual, identity-based systems that can compel acquirers to debit merchants. A confirmed on-chain transaction is irreversible by design. Smart-contract escrow (Coinbase/Shopify Commerce Payments Protocol) handles authorized refunds by honest merchants, but disputes still require a trusted off-chain arbiter — no decentralized substitute for Visa's dispute committee exists at scale. Pseudonymous wallets also prevent the identity-bound enforcement that makes fraud recovery possible. Stripe's stablecoin docs explicitly state: "you won't have disputes that turn into chargebacks."
