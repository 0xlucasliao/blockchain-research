Part 1

Understanding the product

The four capabilities we lead with, each framed as the problem it solves and shown with a simple diagram — no blockchain background needed. A short tour of other features worth knowing follows at the end.

Highlight · Composability

Primitives that snap together

The problem: Launching a real, compliant token normally means bolting together separate contracts and services — slow, fragile, and different every time.

BSC20 is a set of building blocks — a base token, compliance lists, role permissions, payment memos, an optional value multiplier — that combine into exactly the token you need, deployed and fully configured in a single step. Better still, one compliance list can feed a whole family of tokens: a customer clears KYC once and is recognized everywhere.

What we achieve: issuers assemble a production-ready token like Lego, and reuse the same compliance setup across every product they launch.
many building blocks → one token, one step
Highlight · Compliance lists

Blacklist & whitelist, built in

The problem: Regulated money movement needs the ability to screen who can send and receive — but most tokens have no native way to do it, forcing risky workarounds.

Every BSC20 token can enforce address rules directly, in two modes. A blacklist keeps circulation open to everyone and blocks only flagged addresses — the model for a mass-market stablecoin screening sanctioned wallets. A whitelist flips it: closed by default, only approved (e.g. KYC-cleared) addresses can hold or trade — the model for institutional and securities-style assets. Both are optional; a token with neither is simply open.

What we achieve: compliance that satisfies regulators and institutions is a native switch, not a custom engineering project.
green = allowed · gold = approved · grey = not listed
Highlight · Settlement priority

A Payment Lane reserved for BSC20

The problem: When speculation spikes (a meme-coin or NFT rush), it bids up network fees and crowds ordinary payments out of blocks — so transfers and withdrawals stall exactly when markets are busiest.

The Payment Lane sets aside a guaranteed slice of every block for payments during congestion. Our version is scoped on purpose: only BSC20 tokens are registered, and only BSC20 transactions get the reserved space. Everything else keeps competing in the open lane as it does today.

What we achieve: reliable settlement becomes a property of the BSC20 standard itself — a concrete reason to issue and transact on BSC20 rather than elsewhere.
BSC20 payments keep moving while everything else queues
Highlight · Real-world assets

The multiplier

The problem: Asset-backed tokens (funds, treasuries, staking receipts) change in value daily — but updating thousands of balances one by one is impractical and error-prone.

The multiplier moves everyone's value at once with a single number. Each holder's share count never changes; their displayed balance is shares × the multiplier. A fund posts its daily net-asset-value, a stock split doubles it, interest rolls in — one update, applied uniformly to all holders.

What we achieve: tokenized funds and RWAs behave correctly on-chain with no mass airdrops or rebasing hacks. Note: this is asset value, not stablecoin interest — the stablecoin type has no multiplier.
share count fixed · one number scales every holder at once
Also worth knowing

More features that matter

A few other capabilities, in plain terms — the kind of thing that gives BD real talking points with partners.

Payments that reconcile themselves

Problem: When money moves on-chain, matching each payment to the right invoice, order, or customer is manual and error-prone.
Any BSC20 payment can carry a short memo — an invoice number, order ID, or customer reference — recorded as its own record on-chain. Back-office systems read it and match payments automatically. The same works on issuance and redemption.
Achieve: enterprise-grade, self-reconciling payments — a genuine reason for merchants and businesses to settle on BSC20.

Approve without sending a transaction

Problem: Granting a service permission to move your funds normally means sending (and paying for) an on-chain approval yourself.
With a signed approval, the owner just signs a message and anyone can submit it for them — no separate approval transaction from the user.
Achieve: smoother first-time flows and one-click app experiences, with fewer steps that make newcomers give up.

A kill switch you can trust

Problem: If a key is compromised or an exploit appears, you need to stop the bleeding instantly — yet a single "stop everything" button is itself a risk if that key is stolen.
Transfers, minting, and burning can each be paused independently. Crucially, pausing and un-pausing are held by different teams, so a stolen pause key can't also switch things back on to manipulate.
Achieve: a credible safety story for regulators and partners — fast response without a single point of failure.

Freeze & seize, by the book

Problem: Regulated issuers must be able to act on court orders and sanctions — but arbitrary seizure powers are dangerous and hard to trust.
The protocol enforces a strict two-step: an address must first be blacklisted (frozen), and only then can its balance be seized. Every action leaves an on-chain audit record.
Achieve: enforcement that's both effective and auditable — no back doors, every step on the record.
Part 2

Deep dive

The complete method surface for builders and integrators. "Anyone" means no role is required (transfers still pass compliance and pause checks); a gold tag marks a role that must be held to call.

01

Two variants

Every token is one of two variants, chosen at creation. Both share all base methods; each adds a few of its own. You can tell them apart from the address alone — the 11th byte is 00 for Asset, 01 for Stablecoin.

Asset

RWA · funds · equity
  • Decimals selectable 6–18 (fixed at creation)
  • Adds: multiplier, announce (disclosure), batchMint, custom metadata
  • Extra role: OPERATOR

Stablecoin

fiat-pegged coins
  • Fixed 6 decimals; immutable currency code (USD, EUR…)
  • Deliberately narrow: no multiplier, announce, or batchMint
  • Adds only: currency()

06

Policy Registry — compliance

The engine behind the blacklist / whitelist highlight. Compliance lives in a chain-shared registry; each token references policies in four independent slots.

The model: a policy = one address list + one type, living independently (anyone can create one; its admin maintains the list). A token points four compliance slots at whichever policies it wants. Default policy 0 = no restriction, so an unconfigured BSC20 is a fully open ERC-20.

Two policy types

TypeSemanticsUse
BLOCKLIST (blacklist)Open to all; listed addresses are blockedMass-market stablecoin — screen only sanctioned addresses
ALLOWLIST (whitelist)Blocked by default; only listed addresses allowedInstitutional / securities assets — only KYC-cleared addresses hold or trade

Who can do what

ActionWhoNotes
Create a policyAnyone (permissionless)Sets the policy's admin and type at creation.
Add / remove list membersThe policy's adminUp to 64 addresses per batch; one list reusable across many tokens — edit once, all applied instantly.
Transfer policy adminNominate → nominee confirms (2-step)Guards against sending control to a wrong address.
Permanently freeze a policyThe policy's admin (by renouncing)List locked forever — a "genesis list never expands" commitment.
Attach / swap on a token slotThe token's DEFAULT_ADMINHot-swap, effective immediately.

Four compliance scopes

ScopeChecksOnTypical use
TRANSFER_SENDERthe fromtransfer / transferFromFreeze accounts; holder KYC
TRANSFER_RECEIVERthe totransfer / transferFromBlock sanctioned recipients; restrict to eligible investors
TRANSFER_EXECUTORthe caller (when ≠ from)transferFrom onlyOnly licensed brokers/clearers may move client funds
MINT_RECEIVERthe mint tomint / batchMintNew units only to custody / primary subscribers

Not gated by any policy: approve, burn (your own), all queries. Four independent slots = a naturally composable compliance posture.

Worked example A — mass-market USD stablecoin (blacklist)

— one-time —
createPolicy(complianceMultisig, BLOCKLIST)     → policy #5
token.updatePolicy(TRANSFER_SENDER,  5)
token.updatePolicy(TRANSFER_RECEIVER, 5)         # executor / mint-receiver stay 0

— ongoing —
registry.updateBlocklist(5, true, [0xBad1, ...]) # instantly blocked across every token using #5

— court seizure order —
token.burnBlocked(0xBad1, fullBalance)           # only works because 0xBad1 is already blacklisted

Worked example B — institutional tokenized fund (whitelist, Asset)

— one-time —
createPolicy(investorTeam, ALLOWLIST) → #10   # eligible investors
createPolicy(partnerTeam,  ALLOWLIST) → #11   # licensed brokers
createPolicy(opsTeam,      ALLOWLIST) → #12   # custody accounts
updatePolicy(SENDER 10, RECEIVER 10, EXECUTOR 11, MINT_RECEIVER 12)

— ongoing —
updateAllowlist(10, true,  [newInvestor])     # KYC passed → can hold/trade
updateAllowlist(10, false, [exited])          # soft-freeze; balance stays

— second fund —reuse #10/#11/#12  # KYC once, cleared across the whole product family
07

RBAC — roles

Each token has seven built-in roles (eight on Asset), each governing a non-overlapping set of operations, plus arbitrary custom roles.

RoleGovernsSuggested holder
DEFAULT_ADMINGrant/revoke roles, swap policies, supply cap, role hierarchyCold multisig, rarely touched
MINTmint / mintWithMemo / batchMintTreasury / issuance automation
BURNburn (self only)Redemption system
BURN_BLOCKEDburnBlocked (seizure)Compliance multisig
PAUSEpauseAuto-monitoring + security (fast)
UNPAUSEunpauseExec multisig (deliberate)
METADATAName / symbol / URIs / custom metadataOperations team
OPERATOR (Asset only)updateMultiplier, announceFund operations / investment mgmt

Plus custom roles (any 32-byte id): no built-in effect, but usable as an on-chain membership roster or to build multi-tier authority via setRoleAdmin.

Four rules worth knowing

#Rule
1Every role has an admin role (default DEFAULT_ADMIN) — you need it to grant that role.
2PAUSE and UNPAUSE are split on purpose: pausing is fast/low-threshold, resuming is slow/high-threshold. A stolen key gets only one-way power.
3The last admin is protected — normal revoke/renounce can't remove it. Only the explicit, irreversible renounceLastAdmin() can.
4Born ownerless is possible: initialAdmin = 0 + everything set in initCalls → a permanently immutable token.
