Unsecured credit on Web3

Unsecured loans are foundational to consumer and SME economies. This note reframes your original paper into the site style and maps the critical path: identity and verifiable income rails first, then portable credit scoring and protocol-level unsecured lending.

Written 29 April 2026 · Last updated 29 April 2026

Credit cards BNPL SME lending Identity + payroll infra

1. Why unsecured credit matters

Unsecured lending converts future cash flow into present purchasing power. It supports household upgrades, operating cash for businesses, and daily checkout flexibility. Without it, a large share of real-economy activity is constrained by current liquidity.

Four archetypes
Credit cards

Spend now, settle at cycle end. Limits generally anchored to income and repayment history.

BNPL

Small initial limits that scale with repayment behavior and default-free track record.

Renovation loans

Capital for home improvements based on repayment capacity, not existing collateral stock.

Small business loans

Working capital based on business model quality, cash flows, and operator history.

2. Progressive trust model (BNPL pattern)

The strongest unsecured model is incremental trust: low initial exposure, then larger limits only after clean repayment. On-chain systems can make this progression transparent and portable across lenders.

Credit ladder
Level 1Starter limit: USD 50-100
Level 2After 3 on-time repayments: around USD 300
Level 3After 6 clean months: around USD 700
Level 4After 12 clean months + income signal: around USD 1,500
Level 5Dynamic limits based on full profile (repayment, income, identity attestations)

3. Structural advantages on Web3

Unsecured credit on-chain is not only cheaper to operate; it can be more open, composable, and globally liquid when core identity and income rails exist.
01
Global liquidity

Pools can aggregate capital cross-border instead of relying on single-jurisdiction balance sheets.

02
Open lender participation

Yield and risk can be distributed to market participants rather than concentrated in bank intermediaries.

03
Composable risk signals

Repayment data, attestations, and identity proofs can be reusable building blocks for new lenders.

04
Portable borrower history

A borrower can carry a verifiable repayment track record across products and platforms.

05
Programmable servicing

Disbursement, schedule logic, and collections workflows become machine-executable policy.

06
Consumer economy expansion

Credit-enabled users can unlock higher conversion in on-chain commerce and service marketplaces.

4. Critical gaps today

Two missing pieces still block unsecured lending from scaling safely in open networks: robust identity and verifiable income.

Traditional finance inputs

  • Government ID and KYC frameworks
  • Credit bureau scores and default records
  • Bank-verified income and payroll history
  • Tax records and compliance reporting

Web3 gaps to close

  • Sybil-resistant, privacy-preserving identity
  • Portable credit score primitives
  • Reliable on-chain income attestations
  • Selective disclosure for compliance checks

Gap 1: Identity

Unsecured credit is credit to a person or operator. Without resilient identity, borrowers can reset risk state through wallet churn.

Gap 2: Income proofs

Limit sizing requires repayment capacity evidence. Payroll and revenue attestations must become machine-verifiable and privacy-preserving.

5. Enabling stack and rollout path

Infrastructure sequence
1
Web3 payroll rails

Salary, contractor, and business revenue signals available on-chain.

2
Privacy-preserving identity

Selective disclosure identity and attribute proofs for underwriting and compliance.

3
Credit scoring primitives

Composable scoring from repayment, income, and identity attestations.

4
Unsecured lending products

BNPL, revolving consumer credit, and SME lines using the above primitives.

5
Scaled consumer adoption

Higher trust, lower default volatility, and broader market participation.

Privacy and verifiability should coexist. Borrowers should prove thresholds (for example, income above a level or no recent defaults) without exposing full transaction history. That unlocks underwriting while preserving user confidentiality.

6. Use case conclusion

Unsecured credit is a high-impact Web3 use case with clear demand and clear blockers. The opportunity is large, but the dependencies are unglamorous: identity and income infrastructure before headline lending products.
